# Rewriting an existing scene

You are revising a scene that was already written. "Current scene" shows it with its beats numbered
from 1. "Issues to fix" lists what reviewers found; "Teacher's instructions for this rewrite", when
present, says what the teacher wants changed — follow those instructions as long as they stay
faithful to the source and the scene's goal.

- Fix every listed issue. Keep the parts that already work: good examples, accurate statements,
  the overall structure and the scene's goal.
- Stay within the same scene type and goal so it still fits between its neighbours.
- If an issue says a fact is unsupported, either ground it in the relevant source or remove it.
- If an issue concerns length, split long beats or trim the board rather than deleting content the
  objectives need.
- Write the complete scene again in the response format (not a list of changes).

# Packaging, header details and flow

- `content.admin_leak` or `flow.packaging`: the scene talks about the source instead of the subject.
  Remove every mention of the video, clip or segment, the source's part numbers, the timings and
  durations of the video or clip itself, the document and the source's authors or reviewers (the
  issue says where: a beat, the title, a board item or the quiz text). Say "today", "in this
  session" or "next" instead, keep the teaching content of the sentence, and never put another name
  in place of a removed one. Durations, dates, people, departments and versions that belong to the
  subject (the duration of a pulse, an activity's duration in a schedule, a protocol version such as
  IPv6) are content: keep them.
- `flow.bridge`: open with one beat that links the previous scene (see "Neighbouring scenes" and
  "This scene" → `bridge_in`) to this scene's idea.
- `flow.repeated_intro` or `content.duplicate_intro`: drop the re-introduction, the repeated
  objectives or the "coming up next" filler, give the scene a title of its own and go straight to
  what it teaches.
- `flow.order`: if the scene uses an idea that is only taught later, explain the little the learner
  needs right here or reword so it no longer depends on it.
